# Subclone derivation from scATAC-seq — R2_Q3

Two documents in one. **Part 1** is the concise paragraph for the manuscript. **Part 2** is
the step-by-step justification: what each operation does and what fails without it. Every
number is produced by `run_all.sh` in this folder.

---

# Part 1 — Methods paragraph (for the paper)

**Subclone calling.** Subclones were derived from the epiAneufinder state matrix (298
shared 5 Mb windows × cell; 0 = loss, 1 = normal, 2 = gain). States were recoded to
−1/0/+1 so that the mean across cells gives a copy-number profile per window,
(fraction of cells called gain) − (fraction called loss). Cells were clustered on the
Euclidean distance between their recoded call vectors, divided by the square root of the
number of windows so that distances express a root-mean-square per-window call difference
and are comparable across tumours, using Ward's criterion on unsquared distances
(`ward.D2`); the primary branch point (k = 2) was taken as the candidate clone boundary.
Because cutree-based splitting returns the requested number of clones whether or not any
structure exists, each candidate boundary was then tested: a copy-number profile was
computed for each branch, the per-window difference between the two profiles was averaged
within every chromosome arm carrying at least five informative windows, and the largest
arm mean was taken as the *separation* of the split. An arm-level criterion was used
because genome-wide profile distance did not discriminate in this cohort (0.117–0.188 for
the primary split of all nine tumours, with the validated P4 subclone the lowest of the
nine); subclonal events here are arm-scale, and averaging within an arm additionally
suppresses the per-window sampling noise generated by low-coverage cells. A split was
accepted only if both branches held ≥ 20 cells and ≥ 5 % of the tumour **and** the
separation was ≥ 0.55; otherwise the tumour was reported as a single clone. All cells were
retained under either outcome. The threshold equals the separation of the P4 chr8q
subclone (0.571), the only subclone with independent spatial validation, so that no split
weaker than an orthogonally confirmed one is reported. Five of nine tumours passed,
yielding 14 clones across 20 076 malignant cells.

*Upstream, for completeness:* malignant-cell fragments were exported per tumour from the
tumour-compartment ArchR project (autosomes only) and copy-number states called with
epiAneufinder v1.1.5 using 5 Mb windows, the ENCODE hg38 blacklist v2,
`BSgenome.Hsapiens.UCSC.hg38`, GC correction, chrX/chrY/chrM excluded, `minFrags = 5000`
and `k = 4`, with package defaults otherwise. Eleven tumours had malignant scATAC-seq;
P3 (21 cells) and P13 (18 cells) were excluded for insufficient cells.

# Part 2 — What each step does, and why it is needed

## Step 0 — what epiAneufinder actually hands over, and what it does not

A 298 × N matrix of states, one call per window per cell. In P4 (2 832 cells):

- **92.9 %** of all calls are "normal", 4.8 % gain, 2.4 % loss
- the median cell carries **19 non-normal calls out of 298 windows**

Two consequences set up everything that follows. First, **a single cell is
uninformative** — a subclone only becomes visible once many cells are pooled. Second,
epiAneufinder stops here: it calls states per cell but does not group cells into clones,
and its `split_subclones()` helper takes the number of clones as a required input and
returns exactly that many. Nothing in the package decides *whether* a tumour is clonally
heterogeneous. That decision is what Steps 4–6 add.

## Step 1 — recode 0/1/2 to −1/0/+1

*What:* `X <- (M == 2) - (M == 0)`, which for these values is `M − 1`.

*Why:* so that averaging the matrix **is** the copy-number profile. On the recoded scale
`rowMeans` gives (fraction gain) − (fraction loss) directly, a value in [−1, 1] that is
zero for a normal genome and signed the way a reader expects. On the raw 0/1/2 scale a
normal genome averages to 1, so every later difference would need re-centring and the sign
would carry no meaning.

*Necessary?* For the distance, no — shifting both cells by the same constant leaves every
pairwise difference identical (checked over 79 800 pairs: maximum discrepancy 0). For
every downstream quantity, yes: the profile, the arm means and the separation are all
averages of these calls.

## Step 2 — Euclidean cell–cell distance, divided by √(number of windows)

*What:* `dist()` on the recoded vectors, then `/ √298`.

*Why the division:* plain Euclidean distance is a *total* over windows and therefore
depends on how many windows the grid happens to have — the same biology on a 1 Mb grid
(~1 500 windows) would give a much larger number. Dividing by √(n windows) moves the sum
inside the square root and turns it into a **root-mean-square difference per window**,
bounded 0–2 and comparable across tumours and window sizes.

*What the number then means:* P4's median cell–cell distance is 0.343. Decoded, two
typical P4 cells disagree at **34 of 298 windows** (11.4 %), essentially all by one state;
√(34/298) = 0.338, matching. Since a single cell carries ~19 non-normal calls, two
independent cells disagreeing at ~34 windows is just 19 + 19 of each cell's own noise
landing in different places. **The median cell–cell distance in these data is noise, not
biology** — which is exactly why the clone decision cannot rest on this distance and has
to be made on the profile difference instead (Steps 4–5).

*Caveat, measured:* the division is not purely cosmetic. The raw distances are heavily
tied (integer-valued squared distances on a 298-window, 3-state grid), and the rescaling
breaks those ties in a different order, changing the partition in 6 of 9 tumours
(ARI 0.68–0.99). Eight of nine **decisions** are unaffected — same arm, separation within
0.02, same accept/reject; P11 is the exception (see the caveat at the end).

## Step 3 — Ward linkage, cut at the primary branch point

*What:* `hclust(..., "ward.D2")`, `cutree(k = 2)`.

*Why Ward:* it produces compact, balanced groups, which matters because the alternative
linkages fail outright on these data — average and complete linkage put a single outlier
cell against the remaining 2 831 at the top merge, which is not a clone boundary.
`ward.D2` rather than `ward.D` because Ward's criterion applies to unsquared distances,
which is what `dist()` returns.

*Why only k = 2:* the question being asked is whether a tumour divides **at all**. Cutting
deeper multiplies untested hypotheses, and every downstream analysis (TF activity, gene
score) consumes a two-group contrast.

*Necessary?* Yes as a *proposal* step — something has to nominate a candidate partition.
It is emphatically not a decision: at this point the split is a candidate and nothing has
yet been checked.

## Step 4 — turn each branch into a copy-number profile

*What:* per window, (fraction of the branch's cells called gain) − (fraction called loss).

*Why:* this is the aggregation that recovers signal from calls that are individually
near-useless. On P4's chr8:115 Mb window, **91 % of the minor branch's cells carry a gain
call against 14 % of the major branch's** — a difference invisible in any one cell and
unambiguous across 612.

*Necessary?* Yes. No per-cell statistic is stable enough to judge a split; the profile is
the first quantity in the chain with usable precision.

## Step 5 — average the profile difference within chromosome arms, take the largest

*What:* subtract the two profiles (298 values), average within each chromosome arm
carrying ≥ 5 informative windows (27 arms of 38 in this grid), take the arm with the
largest absolute mean. For P4: 8q +0.571, then 7q −0.068, 13q +0.061, 9p +0.043,
8q's own partner arm 8p +0.030.

*Why arm-level rather than a genome-wide summary:* because genome-wide summaries do not
discriminate here. The RMS profile difference of the primary split is 0.117–0.188 in **all
nine** tumours and **P4 — the one validated subclone — has the smallest value of the
nine**. Subtracting each tumour's own noise floor does not help (0.112–0.188, P4 still near
the bottom). What separates a real subclone from diffuse noise in this cohort is
**focality, not magnitude**: P4's difference sits on one arm out of 38 while the others are
spread thinly across the genome. A criterion that measures focality is therefore the only
one that can carry a single cohort-wide threshold.

*Why averaging rather than the per-window maximum:* the largest single-window difference
in P4 is 0.770, but a maximum over 298 noisy values is precisely the statistic that
low-coverage cells inflate. epiAneufinder normalises each cell's mean coverage but not its
variance, and a call requires a segment mean to deviate ≥ 50 % from the cell mean, so
shallow cells cross that fixed relative threshold more often — the Spearman correlation
between per-cell fragment count and the fraction of non-normal calls is negative in all
nine tumours (−0.12 to −0.50) and within every clone. An arm mean over ten windows is not
vulnerable to that; a genome-wide maximum is.

*Why the ≥ 5-window rule:* without it, small arms (19p, 22q, 12p) whose mean rests on one
or two windows dominate the maximum. Clustering the cells on arm-level profiles rather
than windows fails for the same reason — it stops recovering P4's chr8q clone entirely
(ARI 0.016 against a targeted chr8q split).

*Necessary?* This is the step that makes the whole procedure possible: it is the only
point at which a number emerges that is comparable across tumours of very different size
and depth, and therefore the only place a single threshold can be set.

## Step 6 — the two acceptance gates

*Size:* both branches ≥ 20 cells and ≥ 5 % of the tumour. A smaller branch has an unstable
profile and its arm mean is dominated by sampling noise.

*Separation:* ≥ **0.55**. This is P4's value (0.571), the only subclone with orthogonal
validation, so nothing weaker than a clone we can independently see in tissue is reported.
The next tumour below is P8 at 0.465, so the threshold sits in a gap rather than on top of
a sample.

*Necessary?* This is the entire point of the exercise. Without it, `cutree(k = 2)` returns
two clones in every tumour whether or not any structure exists.

*Reporting:* accepted splits are labelled `c1` (major) and `c2` (minor) by size, so every
downstream contrast reads as minor clone relative to the bulk of the tumour. A rejected
tumour is reported as one clone with all its cells; no cell is discarded under either
outcome. The mean separation of 20 random splits of the same two sizes is recorded per
tumour as an empirical noise floor [`epi_clone_decision.csv`].

## Worked example: P4, end to end

| step | quantity | value |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | epiAneufinder table | 298 windows × 2 832 cells; 92.9 % normal; 19 non-normal calls in the median cell |
| 2 | median cell–cell RMS distance | 0.343 = 34 of 298 windows disagreeing, i.e. noise |
| 3 | `ward.D2`, k = 2 | 2 220 and 612 cells |
| 4 | profile at chr8:115 Mb | gain in 14 % of c1 cells vs 91 % of c2 cells |
| 5 | arm means of the difference | **8q +0.571**; next is 7q −0.068; chr8p +0.030 |
| 6 | gates | 0.571 ≥ 0.55 ✓ and 612 ≥ max(20, 5 % × 2 832) = 142 ✓ → **two clones** |

The chr8q profile difference along the arm: 0.113 at 50 Mb, 0.668 at 90 Mb, peaking at
0.770 at 115 Mb, falling to 0.475 at 135 Mb — a real event that is strongest distally, not
a flat block, which is why the arm mean (0.571) sits below the peak (0.770).

## Validation of the P4 subclone

Within the scATAC-seq data, the 612 cells of the minor clone carry a chr8q gain fraction
of 0.683 against 0.116 in the major clone (Wilcoxon p = 1.3 × 10⁻²⁸⁶), while chr8p — the
internal control arm — shifts the same way approximately 17-fold less (0.059 vs 0.026); a
whole-chromosome or global artefact would move both arms equally. Against a *targeted*
partition (k-means, k = 2, on the per-cell chr8q gain fraction alone), the unsupervised
genome-wide clustering reaches ARI 0.521 with precision 0.925 and recall 0.634 — it finds
the chr8q clone without being directed at chromosome 8.

Spatially, all four Visium sections are the same tumour (PT811 = P4; A1/B1 fresh, C1/D1
snap-frozen). Malignant spots were scored for chr8q coverage against normal spots on the
same section and split by k-means on that score; Moran's I (6 nearest neighbours,
row-normalised weights, 299 permutations) was 0.902 (A1), 0.794 (B1), 0.760 (C1) and
0.664 (D1), all p < 0.0033 — contiguous territories, on both fresh and snap-frozen
material.

## Caveats to carry into the text

1. **P11 is a borderline call.** Its separation (0.598) is the closest to the threshold,
   and it is the one tumour whose accept/reject flips under an equivalent-but-differently-
   tie-broken clustering (0.497, reject). P4, P5, P10 and P23 are unaffected. Report P11
   as provisional, or drop it.
2. **P5 and P23 peak at the same chr1 windows.** A cross-tumour coincidence of coordinates
   is the signature of a gene-density or mappability confound; check those windows before
   presenting either as a chr1q subclone.
3. **Deeper structure exists and is not reported.** The same threshold applied recursively
   finds further divisions in P4 (9p, within the chr8q clone), P10 (11p, 8q) and P23
   (9q, 4p). Analysis stops at the primary split by design.
